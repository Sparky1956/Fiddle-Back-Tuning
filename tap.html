Tips

  • Hold the plate lightly at a point that barely moves (or rest it on soft foam), tap firmly and briefly, and let it ring.
  • Keep the microphone 5–10 cm from the plate in a quiet room.
  • Do several taps with the same hold and tap points; the average makes the real peaks stand out. Press Clear taps before changing hold or tap position.
  • Choose the target note you are tuning towards. The box shows how far the largest peak is from it: minus means flat, plus means sharp, and 100 cents is one semitone. Green is within 5 cents.
  • A plate has several modes, not one fundamental. Which ones show up depends on where you hold and tap it.
  • Ring time is how long that peak takes to fade by 20 dB. Longer ring time and higher Q mean less damping. These are estimates.
  • A steady peak at 60 or 120 Hz is probably mains hum, not the plate.
  • If the trigger fires on room noise, move the trigger level up. If taps are missed, move it down.
  • Play tap as detected rebuilds the tap from its peaks: their frequencies, levels and ring times. Play tap tuned to plays the same tap shifted so the largest peak lands exactly on the target note, so you can hear the difference. In the table, Tone holds a steady sine and Tap plays a tap of that one peak.
  • On iPhone, played sounds may be quiet while the microphone is on. Stop the microphone to hear them louder.
